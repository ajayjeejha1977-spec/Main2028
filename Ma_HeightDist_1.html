Properties of Triangles — Complete 10-Stage Remember Sheet

Theory from ALLEN Digital "Solution of Triangle" (Parts 01–19) · Practice from Advance Problems in Mathematics, Class-XI (PT-1, PT-2, Ex-1 to Ex-5) · Stages 1–10 · 40 points

Stage 1 — Direct Formula & Ratio Parametrisation S1PT1 Q2,4,7–12,15,17,18 · ALLEN Pt 02,03,13

1Solvable if 3 elements known (at least 1 side). SSS, SAS → cosine rule; ASA, AAS → sine rule. Always use \(A+B+C=180^\circ\) first.
2\(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}=2R\)   \(\cos B=\dfrac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{2ac}\)
3Ratio of angles → ratio of sides (sides ∝ sines). Ratio of sides \(a:b:c=p:q:r\): put \(a=pk,\,b=qk,\,c=rk\); \(k\) cancels in angles. Largest angle is opposite the largest side.
4Short trick: sides as polynomials in \(x\) → substitute a convenient \(x\) (Illus 10: \(x=2\) gives 7, 5, 3, \(\cos\theta=-\tfrac12\)).

Stage 2 — Sine Rule — Substitution & Ambiguous Case S2PT1 Q1,3,5,16 · Ex4 Q19 · Ex5 Q3,8,19 · ALLEN Pt 02, 13(d)

5Sides ↔ sines: put \(a=k\sin A\) (or \(2R\sin A\)) in any identity mixing sides and angles; it collapses via \(\sin(B+C)\sin(B-C)=\sin^2B-\sin^2C\).   \(a+b>c\iff\sin A+\sin B>\sin C\)
6SSA test (angle \(B\), adjacent side \(c\), opposite side \(b\)): \(b<c\sin B\) none; \(b=c\sin B\) one; \(c\sin B<b<c\) two; \(b\ge c\) one. \(\sin\)>1 ⇒ impossible
7Quadratic route: \(a^2-2c\cos B\cdot a+(c^2-b^2)=0\). Count positive roots: product \(<0\) ⇒ exactly one triangle.
8\(\dfrac{\sin A}{\sin C}=\dfrac{\sin(A-B)}{\sin(B-C)}\Rightarrow a^2,b^2,c^2\) in A.P.   Altitude \(p=c\sin B=b\sin C\).

Stage 3 — Cosine Rule & Projection Formula S3PT1 Q2,4,8,12 · PT2 Q5 · Ex2 Q14,19 · Ex3 Q10,15 · Ex4 Q1,4,5,18,23 · ALLEN Pt 03,04

9\(b^2=a^2+c^2-2ac\cos B\). Type test on the largest side: \(a^2=b^2+c^2\) right; \(>\) obtuse; \(<\) acute.
10Projection: \(a=b\cos C+c\cos B\) (and cyclic). Recognise \(a\cos B+b\cos A=c\) instantly.
11\(c\cos^2\tfrac A2+a\cos^2\tfrac C2=\tfrac{3b}{2}\Rightarrow a+c=2b\) (expand \(\cos^2\tfrac A2=\tfrac{1+\cos A}{2}\), then projection).   \((a-b)^2\cos^2\tfrac C2+(a+b)^2\sin^2\tfrac C2=c^2\)
12\(\cot A+\cot C\) over \(\cot B\): use \(\cot A=\dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{4\Delta}\). cos → sides, then simplify

Stage 4 — Napier's Analogy (Tangent Rule) S4PT2 Q6,10,11 · Ex2 Q2 · Ex3 Q5 · Ex4 Q14 · Ex5 Q1,11(a) · ALLEN Pt 05, 13(c)

13\(\tan\dfrac{A-B}{2}=\dfrac{a-b}{a+b}\cot\dfrac C2\) (and cyclic). Trigger: two sides + included angle with awkward numbers.
14Procedure: \(A+B=180^\circ-C\) known → Napier gives \(A-B\) → solve the two linear equations → third side by sine/cosine rule.
15Given \(\cos(A-B)\): \(\cos(A-B)=\dfrac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\) with \(t=\tan\tfrac{A-B}{2}\) → find \(\cot\tfrac C2\) (Illus 17: \(a=6,b=3,\cos(A-B)=\tfrac45\Rightarrow C=90^\circ,\Delta=9\)).
16Napier needs the included angle; \(\tfrac{A-B}{2}\) carries the half — don't drop it.

Stage 5 — Area & Half-Angle Formulas (s-notation) S5PT2 Q1,2,3,8,12–15 · Ex2 Q15,17 · Ex3 Q3,11,12 · ALLEN Pt 06,07

17\(\Delta=\tfrac12ab\sin C=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}=\dfrac{abc}{4R}=rs\).   Altitudes \(p_1:p_2:p_3=\tfrac1a:\tfrac1b:\tfrac1c\) ⇒ altitudes in A.P. ⇒ sides in H.P.
18\(\sin\tfrac A2=\sqrt{\tfrac{(s-b)(s-c)}{bc}}\),   \(\cos\tfrac A2=\sqrt{\tfrac{s(s-a)}{bc}}\),   \(\tan\tfrac A2=\sqrt{\tfrac{(s-b)(s-c)}{s(s-a)}}=\dfrac{\Delta}{s(s-a)}\)
19Use \(\tan\tfrac A2=\dfrac{\Delta}{s(s-a)}\) whenever \(\Delta\) appears.   \((b+c-a)\tan\tfrac A2=\dfrac{2\Delta}{s}\)   \((s-a)(s-b)=s(s-c)\Rightarrow\tan\tfrac C2=1\Rightarrow C=90^\circ\)
20\(16\Delta^2=(a+b+c)(b+c-a)(c+a-b)(a+b-c)=4b^2c^2\sin^2A\).   \(\Delta=a^2-(b-c)^2\Rightarrow\cot\tfrac A2=4\).

Stage 6 — Circumradius R & Inradius r S6PT1 Q19,20 · PT2 Q16–20 · Ex1 Q2,5,11 · Ex2 Q8,9,11 · Ex3 Q1,6,13,14 · Ex4 Q6,12,17,20,21 · ALLEN Pt 09

21\(R=\dfrac{abc}{4\Delta}\)   \(r=\dfrac{\Delta}{s}=(s-a)\tan\tfrac A2=4R\sin\tfrac A2\sin\tfrac B2\sin\tfrac C2\)   \(\dfrac Rr=\dfrac{abc}{4(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\)
22Right triangle: \(R=\tfrac{c}{2}\), \(r=\tfrac{a+b-c}{2}\), \(a^2+b^2+c^2=8R^2\). Equilateral: \(R=2r\), \(a^2+b^2+c^2=9R^2\).
23\(a\cot A+b\cot B+c\cot C=2(R+r)\)   \(\cos A+\cos B+\cos C=1+\tfrac rR\)   \(\sum\cos^2\tfrac A2=2+\tfrac{r}{2R}\)   \(s=4R\prod\cos\tfrac A2\)
24Convert to R whenever sides meet trig ratios: \(a=2R\sin A\). Distances: \(O\) to side \(=R\cos A\); \(I\) to vertex \(=r\,\mathrm{cosec}\tfrac A2\).

Stage 7 — Exradii r₁, r₂, r₃ S7Ex1 Q1,3,4,6–10,12–14 · Ex3 Q8,9 · Ex4 Q2 · Ex5 Q4,6,17,22 · ALLEN Pt 09

25\(r_1=\dfrac{\Delta}{s-a}=s\tan\tfrac A2=4R\sin\tfrac A2\cos\tfrac B2\cos\tfrac C2\) (cyclic). First move: write every \(r_i\) as \(\Delta/(s-\cdot)\).
26\(r_1+r_2+r_3-r=4R\)   \(rr_1r_2r_3=\Delta^2\)   \(r_1r_2+r_2r_3+r_3r_1=s^2\)   \(\dfrac1r=\dfrac1{r_1}+\dfrac1{r_2}+\dfrac1{r_3}\)
27Ratio given (\(r_1=2r_2=3r_3\)): \(\tfrac{1}{s-a}=\tfrac{2}{s-b}=\tfrac{3}{s-c}=k\) → add to get \(s\) → sides.   \(r_1>r_2>r_3\iff a>b>c\).
28\(r_1,r_2,r_3\) in H.P. \(\iff s-a,s-b,s-c\) in A.P. \(\iff a,b,c\) in A.P.   Equilateral: \(r:R:r_1=1:2:3\).

Stage 8 — A.P. / G.P. / H.P. Conditions S8PT2 Q4,7 · Ex2 Q5,7,12 · Ex4 Q4,7,10,11 · Ex5 Q2,13,24 · ALLEN Illus 2,5,15,18,38,45,57

29Write the condition first: \(a,b,c\) A.P. \(\Rightarrow 2b=a+c\); H.P. ⇒ take reciprocals. \(a,b,c\) A.P. \(\iff\sin A,\sin B,\sin C\) A.P.
30Angles in A.P. ⇒ \(B=60^\circ\) and \(b^2=a^2+c^2-ac\).   \(a^2,b^2,c^2\) A.P. \(\iff\cot A,\cot B,\cot C\) A.P.
31Sides A.P. with greatest angle \(=2\times\) smallest ⇒ \(4:5:6\).   Altitudes H.P. \(\iff\) sides A.P.
32Different target progression: convert to a common form (sines / cotangents / \(s-a\)), then use \(\sin(A+B)\sin(A-B)\) factoring.

Stage 9 — Cevians, Cyclic Quadrilaterals & Regular Polygons S9PT2 Q9 · Ex2 Q3,4,6,8,10,13,20 · Ex3 Q4 · Ex4 Q3,8 · Ex5 Q11(b),12(b),20 · ALLEN Pt 08,10,11,12

33m–n theorem: \(BD:DC=m:n\), \(\angle ADC=\theta\), \(\angle BAD=\alpha\), \(\angle DAC=\beta\): \((m+n)\cot\theta=m\cot\alpha-n\cot\beta=n\cot B-m\cot C\).
34Lengths: median \(AM=\tfrac12\sqrt{2b^2+2c^2-a^2}\); bisector \(AD=\dfrac{2bc}{b+c}\cos\tfrac A2\); \(4(m_a^2+m_b^2+m_c^2)=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)\).
35Cyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles sum to \(180^\circ\) (cosines negate) — apply cosine rule in both triangles on the shared diagonal; \(\text{Area}=\sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\); Ptolemy.
36Regular \(n\)-gon: inscribed \(a=2R\sin\tfrac\pi n\), area \(=\tfrac n2R^2\sin\tfrac{2\pi}n\); circumscribed \(a=2r\tan\tfrac\pi n\), area \(=nr^2\tan\tfrac\pi n\). Area ratio of triangles on one base \(=\) ratio of bases.

Stage 10 — JEE Hard — Inequalities, Euler Line, Coordinates S10Ex4 Q9,16,22,24,25 · Ex5 Q7,22 · ALLEN Illus 20,52,53,59,63,64, Pt 12,18–19

37AM–GM on \(s-a,s-b,s-c\): \(\Delta\le\dfrac{s^2}{3\sqrt3}\); \(\dfrac{r_1r_2r_3}{r^3}\ge27\) (equality: equilateral). \(r\le\dfrac R2\).
38Orthocentre \(H\): \(AH=2R|\cos A|\); distance from \(H\) to \(BC=2R\cos B\cos C\). Euler line: \(O,G,H\) collinear, \(OG:GH=1:2\), so \(O=\tfrac{3G-H}{2}\).
39\(H,O\) equidistant from \(BC\) on the same side \(\Rightarrow\tan B\tan C=3\). Orthic triangle: sides \(a\cos A,\ b\cos B,\ c\cos C\); angles \(\pi-2A,\pi-2B,\pi-2C\).
40Coordinates: find the centres / side lengths first (equidistance, shoelace), then use the \(R,r,\Delta\) formulas. Existence: check triangle inequality on all three pairs.
