Compound Angles — Complete 10-Stage Remember Sheet

Practice from Advance Problems in Mathematics, Class-XI (Chapter 1: PT-1, PT-2, Ex-1 to Ex-5) · Formulas organised by solving approach · 40 points

Stage 1 — Reduction, Quadrants & Ranges S1PT1 Q2–5,10 · PT2 · Ex4 Q9

1Quadrant signs (ASTC): reduce \(n\cdot90^\circ\pm\theta\): even \(n\) keep the function, odd \(n\) swap sin↔cos, tan↔cot; sign from the quadrant of the original angle.
2Periods: \(\sin,\cos\) \(2\pi\); \(\tan,\cot\) \(\pi\). \(\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta\), \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\). Strip multiples of 360° first (e.g. \(\tan(-1125^\circ)=\tan(-45^\circ)\)).
3Ranges: \(|\sin|,|\cos|\le1\); \(|\sec|,|\mathrm{cosec}|\ge1\); \(\tan,\cot\) any real. \(\cos\theta=\tfrac{1+t^2}{1-t^2}\) is impossible for \(t\ne0\).
4Trig equations: factor as a quadratic in \(\sin x\) (e.g. \(2s^2+3s+1=0\)), list all roots in the interval, then add. Compare numbers by converting each to a decimal (angle in radians: \(5\pi/4\approx3.93\)).

Stage 2 — Identities & Simplification S2PT1 Q1,6–9 · Ex3 Q2 · Ex4 Q13,14,18,43

5\(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\), \(1+\tan^2=\sec^2\), \(1+\cot^2=\mathrm{cosec}^2\). Convert everything to \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) first; \((\sec\theta-\cos\theta)=\tfrac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta}\).
6\(\sin^4+\cos^4=1-2s^2c^2\),   \(\sin^6+\cos^6=1-3s^2c^2\) (\(s^2c^2=p\)). Express higher powers through \(p\) or through one variable.
7\(T_n=\sin^n+\cos^n\): \(T_5-T_3=-s^2c^2T_1\), \(T_7-T_5=-s^2c^2T_3\). Factor out the common block before cancelling.
8Given a relation like \(\sin x+\sin^2x=1\) ⇒ \(\sin x=\cos^2x\) ⇒ \(\tan^2x=\sin x\): set \(t=\sin x\), reduce to a polynomial in \(t\), then use \(t^2+t=1\). Angles like \(6\pi-\alpha,\ 2\pi+\alpha\) vanish first.

Stage 3 — Compound Angle Formulas S3PT2 Q7,8,12 · Ex2 Q1 · Ex3 Q5 · Ex4 Q3,10,12,19,35

9\(\sin(A\pm B)=\sin A\cos B\pm\cos A\sin B\); \(\cos(A\pm B)=\cos A\cos B\mp\sin A\sin B\); \(\tan(A\pm B)=\dfrac{\tan A\pm\tan B}{1\mp\tan A\tan B}\).
10Find the missing ratio first, with its sign from the quadrant (e.g. \(\sin A=\tfrac35\), \(A\) in II ⇒ \(\cos A=-\tfrac45\)). Then substitute.
11Two sums given: square and add: \((\sin\alpha+\sin\beta)^2+(\cos\alpha+\cos\beta)^2=2+2\cos(\alpha-\beta)\); for \(3\sin P+4\cos Q=6,\ 4\sin Q+3\cos P=1\) get \(\sin(P+Q)=\tfrac12\) and reject the impossible case.
12Build the target angle: \(2\alpha=(\alpha+\beta)+(\alpha-\beta)\), then use \(\tan(A+B)\). Quadrant of \(\alpha+\beta\): compute the actual angle (e.g. \(225^\circ+126.9^\circ\) ⇒ IV).

Stage 4 — Transformation: Sum ↔ Product S4PT2 Q2–6,10 · Ex1 Q5,10 · Ex2 Q8 · Ex3 Q1,5,9 · Ex4 Q36,39,46

13\(\sin C+\sin D=2\sin\tfrac{C+D}2\cos\tfrac{C-D}2\); \(\cos C+\cos D=2\cos\tfrac{C+D}2\cos\tfrac{C-D}2\); \(2\sin A\cos B=\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\).
14Complementary pairs: \(\sin34^\circ=\cos56^\circ\), \(\cos68^\circ=\sin22^\circ\), \(\tan20^\circ\cdot\cot70^\circ=1\). Spot them to collapse a product to \(\tfrac12\sin2x\).
15If \(A+B=45^\circ\): \(\tan A+\tan B+\tan A\tan B=1\). \(\tan9^\circ-\tan27^\circ-\tan63^\circ+\tan81^\circ=4\). \(\tan20^\circ+4\sin20^\circ=\sqrt3\).
16Ratio form: \(3\tan\tfrac{x+y}2=5\tan\tfrac{x-y}2\) ⇒ \(\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin y}=\dfrac{5+3}{5-3}\) (use \(\tfrac{\sin x+\sin y}{\sin x-\sin y}=\tfrac{\tan\frac{x+y}2}{\tan\frac{x-y}2}\)).

Stage 5 — Multiple, Half Angles & Power Reduction S5PT2 Q12 · Ex1 Q3,4 · Ex3 Q3,4 · Ex4 Q5,18,28

17\(\sin2A=\tfrac{2t}{1+t^2}\), \(\cos2A=\tfrac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\), \(\tan2A=\tfrac{2t}{1-t^2}\) with \(t=\tan A\). Half-angle sign: from the quadrant of \(x/2\), not of \(x\).
18\(\sin3A=3\sin A-4\sin^3A\), \(\cos3A=4\cos^3A-3\cos A\); \(\cos2A=2\cos^2A-1=1-2\sin^2A\); \(\cos^2A=\tfrac{1+\cos2A}2\).
19\(\sin x\cos^3x-\cos x\sin^3x=\tfrac12\sin2x\cos2x=\tfrac14\sin4x\). Pull out \(\sin x\cos x\) and fold into a multiple angle.
20Linear equation \(a\sin x+b\cos x=c\): divide by \(\sin x\) to get \((a+b\cot x)^2=c^2(1+\cot^2x)\) — a quadratic in \(\cot x\); sum of roots by Vieta. Squaring may add extraneous roots, so check.

Stage 6 — Special Angles & Surd Values S6Ex1 Q11,12,16 · Ex3 Q15 · Ex4 Q27,30,32

21\(\sin18^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt5-1}{4}\),   \(\cos36^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt5+1}{4}\),   \(\tan15^\circ=2-\sqrt3\),   \(\mathrm{cosec}18^\circ=\sqrt5+1\) (root of \(x^2-2x-4=0\)).
22\(\cot7\tfrac12^\circ=\sqrt2+\sqrt3+2+\sqrt6\); \(\tan\tfrac\pi{24}=(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)(\sqrt2-1)\). Derive by repeated \(\cot\tfrac\theta2=\cot\theta+\mathrm{cosec}\,\theta\).
23\(\sin x\sin(60^\circ-x)\sin(60^\circ+x)=\tfrac14\sin3x\) ⇒ \(\sin25^\circ\sin35^\circ\sin85^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{16}\).
24Verify a surd answer numerically (e.g. \(\cot7.5^\circ\approx7.596\)) before choosing among close options. Match \(\sqrt p+\sqrt q+\sqrt r+\sqrt s\) by writing \(2=\sqrt4\).

Stage 7 — Product Chains & Roots of Unity S7Ex1 Q2,17 · Ex2 Q4,9 · Ex3 Q6 · Ex4 Q29,31,33,34,37,38

25\(\cos\theta\cos2\theta\cos4\theta\cdots\cos2^{n-1}\theta=\dfrac{\sin2^n\theta}{2^n\sin\theta}\) (multiply and divide by \(\sin\theta\)).
26\(4\cos^2x-1=\dfrac{\sin3x}{\sin x}\) ⇒ \(\prod(4\cos^2 3^kx-1)\) telescopes. \(\sin\tfrac\pi{22}\sin\tfrac{3\pi}{22}\cdots\) odd-multiple products \(=2^{-m}\).
27\(\cos\tfrac{2\pi}7+\cos\tfrac{4\pi}7+\cos\tfrac{6\pi}7=-\tfrac12\); \(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\cos\tfrac{2k\pi}{2n+1}=-\tfrac12\). \((1+\tan1^\circ)(1+\tan44^\circ)=2\) ⇒ pair \(A+B=45^\circ\).
28Pair first: \(\sin(\pi-x)=\sin x\), \(\cos(\pi-x)=-\cos x\); \(\sin\tfrac\pi8\sin\tfrac{7\pi}8=\sin^2\tfrac\pi8\). Check by decimals — the answer key can have misprints.

Stage 8 — Maxima, Minima & Range S8Ex1 Q1 · Ex2 Q7,15 · Ex3 Q7,8,10,12 · Ex4 Q4,11 · Ex5 Q3,9

29\(a\cos\theta+b\sin\theta\in[-\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\ \sqrt{a^2+b^2}]\). Reduce \(10\cos^2x-6\sin x\cos x+2\sin^2x=6+4\cos2x-3\sin2x\in[1,11]\).
30Substitute \(t=\sin x\) (or \(\cos x\)) on \([-1,1]\); a quadratic in \(t\) is largest/smallest at the vertex or an endpoint (\(f=2c-c^2,\ c\in[-1,1]\Rightarrow[-3,1]\)).
31AM–GM / Cauchy: \(9\sec^2+16\mathrm{cosec}^2\ge(3+4)^2=49\); \(9u+\tfrac4u\ge12\); \(x^2+y^2\) with \(x=r\cos\theta,y=r\sin\theta\) turns constraints into \(r^2\cdot(\ldots)\).
32Reduce power forms via \(s^2c^2\in[0,\tfrac14]\). Check that the equality condition is attainable in the given domain.

Stage 9 — Telescoping, A+B+C=π & Regular Polygons S9Ex1 Q14,15,18,19 · Ex2 Q11,12 · Ex4 Q1,9,45 · Ex5 Q2,11,12,15,19

33\(\dfrac{1}{\sin a\sin(a+d)}=\dfrac{\cot a-\cot(a+d)}{\sin d}\) — the telescoping key; sum collapses to two end terms (reduce \(\cot(x+2\pi)\) etc.).
34If \(A+B+C=\pi\): \(\tan A+\tan B+\tan C=\tan A\tan B\tan C\); \(\sum\cot\tfrac A2=\prod\cot\tfrac A2\); \(\sin4A+\sin4B+\sin4C=0\iff\) right triangle.
35Regular \(n\)-gon: chord \(A_1A_k=2R\sin\tfrac{(k-1)\pi}{n}\); \(\sum A_1A_k^2=2nR^2\); \(r=\tfrac{a}{2}\cot\tfrac\pi n,\ R=\tfrac a2\mathrm{cosec}\tfrac\pi n\Rightarrow r+R=\tfrac a2\cot\tfrac\pi{2n}\); \(\tfrac rR=\cos\tfrac\pi n\).
36\(\tfrac1{\sin(\pi/n)}=\tfrac1{\sin(2\pi/n)}+\tfrac1{\sin(3\pi/n)}\Rightarrow n=7\) (same equation as Ex1 Q19). Reduce the sum using symmetry \(\cot(\pi-x)=-\cot x\).

Stage 10 — JEE Hard: Equations, Heights & Distances, Constraints S10Ex3 Q11,13,14 · Ex4 Q2,6,7,15–17,21,24–26,41,44 · Ex5 Q13,14,16,17

37Factor a mixed equation: \(4\sin x\cos y+2\sin x+2\cos y+1=(2\sin x+1)(2\cos y+1)\). Equality cases: \(\cos x+\cos y-\cos(x+y)=\tfrac32\) holds only at \(x=y=\tfrac\pi3\).
38Heights & distances: draw the right triangles, write \(h\) in terms of each ground distance, equate; for two angles at one point use \(\tan(\theta_1-\theta_2)\) (tower with midpoint: \(\tan\beta=\tfrac12\!\to\!\tfrac14\Rightarrow\tfrac29\)).
39Eliminate \(\theta\)/parameter: express \(x,y\) in \(\tan\theta\), clear denominators to get \(xy+\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma=0\). Roots \(\tan\alpha,\tan\beta\): \(\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\tfrac{\text{sum}}{1-\text{product}}\).
40Exam traps: floor/greatest-integer answers, domain limits on \(\alpha,\beta\), and multiple-correct options — test each option separately; verify \(\sec\theta+\tan\theta=12\Rightarrow\sec\theta=\tfrac{145}{24}\).
