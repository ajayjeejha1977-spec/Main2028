Trigonometric Equations — Targeted Drill: Remember Sheet

Built from the Ex-1 to Ex-4 attempt: the approaches behind the questions circled as unsolved · 24 key points + methods, tips and common mistakes for every group

Group 1 — Counting Solutions in an Interval G1Ex2 Q5, Q8, Q10 · Ex3 Q12, Q14, Q15, Q24, Q26, Q27, Q29, Q47, Q50 · Ex4 Q4–Q6, Q10

1Reduce first to a basic equation (\(\sin2x=\pm\tfrac12\), \(\cos2x=1\), \(\sin^2x=\tfrac14\)), then count: solutions per period × number of periods.
2Periods: \(\sin x,\cos x:2\pi\); \(\tan x:\pi\); \(\sin^2x,|\sin x|,\sin2x:\pi\); \(\sin kx:\tfrac{2\pi}k\). Always say how many periods fit in the interval.
3Mixed equations (\(x+2\tan x=\tfrac\pi2\)): rewrite as \(\tan x=\tfrac{\pi/2-x}2\) and sketch both curves on every branch (Ex3 Q14: 3 roots).
4Exclude the points where tan, a denominator or a log is undefined, and count the end points of the interval separately.
🧠 How to think — step by step
  1. What is the interval's length in periods? Write it down.
  2. Reduce the equation to basic equations; count solutions of each per period.
  3. Add up, then remove the excluded points and double-count nothing.
🛠 Methods & techniques
  • For \(f(x)=g(x)\) with different types (\(x\) and \(\tan x\)), plot both on each branch.
  • Substitution (\(t=16^{\sin^2x}\), \(c=\cos2x\)) turns it into a quadratic; count each root separately.
  • Use symmetry: if \(\theta\) works so do \(\pi-\theta,\ \pi+\theta,\ 2\pi-\theta\) (check).
💡 Tips & tricks
  • Number of solutions of \(\sin kx=c\) (|c|<1) in \([0,2\pi]\) is \(2k\).
  • A quick sketch beats algebra for \(x+2\tan x=\tfrac\pi2\).
⚠ Common mistakes — and how to avoid them
  • Counting only [0, 2π] when the interval is longer → count periods explicitly.
  • Forgetting the modulus (±) → check both signs.
  • Counting a solution at a point where tan is undefined → test the domain.

Group 2 — Sum of Solutions & Systems G2Ex3 Q16, Q31, Q32, Q34, Q36, Q39 · Ex4 Q11

5Factorise with sum-to-product to list every root (Ex3 Q16: \(4\sin\tfrac{5x}2\cos x\cos\tfrac x2=0\), sum \(9\pi\)).
6Systems: solve each equation for the same variable and keep the common values (Ex3 Q31: \(\sin\theta=-\tfrac12\), sum \(3\pi\)).
7Nested sets (Ex3 Q32): find the set \(S\) first, then evaluate the second quantity for each element.
8Sums over a function of the roots (Ex3 Q36): list each root, evaluate the term (\(\tan^2\tfrac x3\)) and add; use symmetry \(\pm x\).
🧠 How to think — step by step
  1. Is it one equation (factorise) or a system (common value)?
  2. List all roots; then check that each lies in the interval.
  3. Only then add or substitute into the required expression.
🛠 Methods & techniques
  • Sum-to-product: \(\sin x+\sin4x=2\sin\tfrac{5x}2\cos\tfrac{3x}2\), \(\sin2x+\sin3x=2\sin\tfrac{5x}2\cos\tfrac x2\).
  • System: solve for \(\sin\theta\) (or \(\cos\theta\)) in both equations and intersect the solution sets.
  • Nested sets: find \(S\), then for each \(\theta\in S\) evaluate the second expression.
💡 Tips & tricks
  • Write the roots in a table before adding.
  • If a sum looks symmetric about π, roots pair to give π.
⚠ Common mistakes — and how to avoid them
  • Missing a factor's roots (x = π from cos(x/2) = 0) → solve each factor separately.
  • Keeping a value that satisfies only one equation of the system.
  • Summing for one θ and not over all of S.

Group 3 — Hidden Quadratic & Substitution G3Ex1 Q7 · Ex3 Q18, Q22, Q23, Q25, Q26, Q27, Q38

9One function: \(\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x\), \(\cos^2\theta=\tfrac{1+\cos2\theta}2\), \(\mathrm{cosec}^2x=\tfrac1{\sin^2x}\); then substitute \(y\).
10Solve the quadratic in \(y\) and reject values outside the range of the function (\(|y|>1\), \(y<0\) for a square).
11Count per period, then over the interval (Ex3 Q26: \(\cos2\theta=0,-\tfrac13\) each give 16 solutions in \([-4\pi,4\pi]\)).
12Special values: \(\sin18^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt5-1}4\), \(\cos36^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt5+1}4\), \(\mathrm{cosec}\,18^\circ=\sqrt5+1\).
🧠 How to think — step by step
  1. Reduce to one trig function.
  2. Solve the quadratic in \(y\); discard values outside the range.
  3. Count solutions per period for each admissible value.
🛠 Methods & techniques
  • Identities: \(\cos2\theta=2\cos^2\theta-1\), \(\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x\).
  • Clear denominators only after noting the excluded points.
  • Unit-circle counting: each value of \(\cos2\theta\) in (−1,1) gives 2 solutions of \(2\theta\) per \(2\pi\).
💡 Tips & tricks
  • Check each root numerically.
  • Remember special angle values (18°, 36°).
⚠ Common mistakes — and how to avoid them
  • Keeping y > 1 or y < 0 for a square.
  • Counting only the solutions with sin x > 0.
  • Mixing up the period of θ and 2θ.

Group 4 — Exponential, Log & Modulus Hybrids G4Ex1 Q3, Q5, Q6 · Ex2 Q5, Q10, Q11 · Ex3 Q10, Q19, Q20, Q23, Q36, Q43

13\(a^{f}+a^{g}\) with \(f+g\) constant: \(u+\tfrac ku=c\) (Ex2 Q5: \(t+\tfrac{16}t=10\); Ex3 Q23: \(u+\tfrac{64}u=16\)).
14Range of the new variable: \(u=e^{\sin x}\in[\tfrac1e,e]\) — test every root (Ex3 Q43: \(1+\sqrt3>e\), no solution).
15Modulus: split on the sign of the expression inside (Ex3 Q19: \(\cot x<0\) gives \(x=\tfrac{2\pi}3\) only).
16Logs: combine them, then check the domain; for a minimum of \(\tfrac4s+\tfrac1{1-s}\) use the derivative: \(s=\tfrac23\), value 9.
🧠 How to think — step by step
  1. Which part is exponential, log or modulus? Remove it first (substitute, split cases).
  2. Find the range of the new variable.
  3. Solve, test every root against the range, count.
🛠 Methods & techniques
  • \(u+\tfrac ku=c\Rightarrow u^2-cu+k=0\).
  • Modulus: case on the sign inside; keep only roots consistent with the case.
  • Optimisation: derivative or AM–GM, then check attainability.
💡 Tips & tricks
  • Compare a root with \(e\approx2.718\) numerically.
  • A decimal check of the final root avoids sign mistakes.
⚠ Common mistakes — and how to avoid them
  • Accepting a root outside the range of the substituted variable.
  • Accepting a case root that breaks the case condition (cot x > 0).
  • Guessing the minimum at the symmetric point.

Group 5 — Parameter & Range of Values G5Ex1 Q9, Q12 · Ex2 Q14 · Ex3 Q11, Q22, Q35, Q45

17\"Has a solution\" ⇔ parameter in the range: solve for the parameter in terms of \(s=\sin x\) and use \(s\in[-1,1]\) (Ex3 Q11: \(\alpha=4+2s\), \([2,6]\)).
18\(a\sin x+b\cos x=c\) is solvable iff \(|c|\le\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) (Ex3 Q22: \(k+1\in[-5,5]\), 11 integers).
19Quadratic in \(y=\sin^2x\in[0,1]\): check the vertex and both end points (Ex3 Q35: \([-\tfrac32,-1]\)).
20Count integers by writing the exact interval first, then check whether the end values are allowed.
🧠 How to think — step by step
  1. Solve for the parameter as a function of \(s=\sin x\) (or \(y=\sin^2x\)).
  2. Take \(s\in[-1,1]\) (or \(y\in[0,1]\)) and find the range.
  3. Convert the range to the answer form (interval, number of integers).
🛠 Methods & techniques
  • Equation linear in \(\sin x\): \(\alpha\) as a linear function of \(s\).
  • Amplitude bound for \(a\sin x+b\cos x\).
  • Quadratic: vertex and end values on the allowed interval.
💡 Tips & tricks
  • Check an end value of the parameter by substituting back.
  • Integers in \([-6,4]\): 11, not 10.
⚠ Common mistakes — and how to avoid them
  • Sign slips when isolating the parameter.
  • Using the wrong interval for s or y.
  • Excluding end points that are allowed.

Group 6 — Bounds, No-Solution & Special Forms G6Ex2 Q11 · Ex3 Q37, Q41, Q44 · Ex4 Q7, Q9

21Prove no solution by bounding: the cubic \(4c^3+4c^2+4c-13\le-1\) on \([-1,1]\) (Ex3 Q41); \(\max(\sin x+2\sin2x-\sin3x)\approx2.73<3\) (Ex4 Q9).
22Compare two sets by solving both: \(\tan\theta=1+\sqrt2\) for each, so \(P=Q\) (Ex4 Q7).
23Locus through intercepts: midpoint \((\tfrac7{2\cos\theta},\tfrac7{2\sin\theta})\) (Ex3 Q37).
24Linear trig equation \(4\cos\theta+5\sin\theta=1\): divide by \(\cos\theta\), square, and select the root by the sign of \(\sec\theta\) (Ex3 Q44).
🧠 How to think — step by step
  1. Can I bound each side so equality is impossible?
  2. Can two sets be compared by solving each one?
  3. Is there a hidden geometric meaning (intercepts, midpoints)?
🛠 Methods & techniques
  • Bound a polynomial in \(\cos x\) by evaluating at \(\pm1\) and checking monotonic behaviour.
  • Divide by \(\cos\theta\) to get \(\tan\theta\) and compare sets.
  • Square a linear trig equation, then reject the extraneous root.
💡 Tips & tricks
  • Rationalise \(1/(\sqrt2-1)=\sqrt2+1\) before comparing.
  • After squaring, always substitute back.
⚠ Common mistakes — and how to avoid them
  • Not rationalising, so equal values look different.
  • Taking intercepts as 7cosθ, 7sinθ instead of 7/cosθ, 7/sinθ.
  • Keeping both roots after squaring.
